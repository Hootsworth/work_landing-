Remote desktop,with no onein the middle.

LinkUp connects two devices directly. Your screen, mouse, keyboard and files never get piped through a corporate server. Built with Rust, Tauri and WebRTC for smooth, end-to-end encrypted control.

Remote desktop usually means your screen, your keystrokes and your files take a detour through someone else’s servers. LinkUp skips the detour. It connects two devices directly, encrypted end to end, for smooth 60fps control.

From “pair” to pixels.

A direct handshake first

On the same local network, devices pair over a direct local HTTP loop on port 8081. No signaling servers involved.

Otherwise, WebRTC signaling

Not on the same network? Signaling helps the two devices find each other, then steps out of the way.

It adapts as the network changes

The host tracks bandwidth, framerate, resolution and ping, and adjusts the video to keep up.

Everything rides the same link

Video, audio, clipboard, files, mouse and keyboard all travel peer to peer.

LinkUp Direct-Sync Handshake

On the same network, or with direct routing, devices connect instantly over a local HTTP pairing loop on port 8081, bypassing signaling servers entirely.

Dynamic WebRTC adaption

Tracks bandwidth, framerate, resolution and ping, and adapts the host’s video to match.

P2P file transfer

64KB chunks with data channel flow control and a visual progress overlay.

Native clipboard sync

A native Rust thread watches clipboard changes in the background.

Pointer lock capture

Lock the cursor in the viewport and translate real movement into absolute coordinates on the host.

System loopback audio

Stream the host’s system audio alongside the video.

JPEG fallback stream

If WebRTC video tracks are blocked, it falls back to a binary JPEG stream over Tauri IPC.
