A screenplay is a blueprint made of words. Between the draft and the floor are a hundred quiet decisions—about rhythm, lenses, light, and who is looking at whom. Introspective gives you a calm table to lay those pieces out.

From words to a working plan.

Break the script

Parses Fountain and standard scripts offline into scenes, dialogue, and character entities in milliseconds.

Read the room

Maps emotional pacing, dramatic tension, and character co-occurrence across the draft.

Plan the coverage

Shapes a starting shot list with focal lengths, lighting notes, and estimated screen time.

Board & export

Connects to local Flux or ComfyUI for storyboards and animatics, then exports clean PDF, JSON, or ZIP packages.

Three ways to run it.

Local Only

100% offline on your machine.

Powered by local Ollama models (llama3.2, mistral, qwen2.5) and local ComfyUI, Automatic1111, or Flux Klein 4B. Not a single line of dialogue leaves your computer.

Hybrid

Local first, cloud when needed.

Runs local inference by default and falls back to OpenAI or Gemini only on complex queries—backed by content-hashed SQLite caching so unchanged scenes are never re-run.

Cloud Only

Zero local GPU load.

Connects directly to GPT-4o or Gemini 2.5 Flash. Your API keys are encrypted at rest on disk using PBKDF2 and symmetric Fernet in keystore.enc.

Why it exists • A personal note

For the space between a feeling and showing it.

There is a particular kind of tired that comes from making a film. It is not just the long days. It is the tabs left open, the versions named final_final_2, and the scene that is still living in your head because there has not been time to explain it to anyone else.

We made Introspective for that quiet space between having a feeling and being ready to show it. A place to put the screenplay, pull the pieces into view, and start making decisions without having to hold the whole film in your head at once.

It can help with the practical things: finding scenes, gathering characters, shaping a shot list, making notes, and seeing where the story has energy. Those things are important. They are also not the reason you started making films.

Use the tool to get closer to the part that only you can make.

If a suggestion gives you a better idea, keep going. If it feels wrong, ignore it. If the strange version is the honest version, choose the strange version. The point is not to make every decision faster. It is to leave you with more attention for the decisions that matter.

Thanks for trusting us with a small corner of your process. We hope it gives you a little room back.

Keep making the thing. — The people building Introspective
What we try to remember

Three promises to keep the tool in its place.

Built for directors, cinematographers, writers, and producers.

  1. 01 Your taste comes first.

    A suggestion is only useful if it helps you see your own idea more clearly. Keep it, change it, or throw it away. The final call is always yours.

  2. 02 The busywork should stay busywork.

    Sorting scenes, checking continuity, building a shot list, and preparing a deck matter. They just should not take the best hours of your day.

  3. 03 Your pages belong to you.

    Your half-finished draft, odd little detail, and story you are not ready to share are yours. Introspective keeps your work close and your choices private.

Run it on your desk.
