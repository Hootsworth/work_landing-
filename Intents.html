Click anywhere to let your mind wander

The modern web is not built for continuity. You open a tab for one reason, get pulled toward another, and five minutes later you’re somewhere else entirely. Not because you’re careless, but because the web is optimised for discovery, not memory.

Four small ways back.

Hold that thought

Select any text and save it, with your own note if you like. Later you return to the exact page and section, with the text highlighted.

Context ladder

A light trail shows where you started and how you got here. One click takes you back to the original context.

Isolate mode

Links are hidden, the noise is removed and the content is reflowed for reading. It’s temporary and reversible.

Contextualise

Select a term and a short explanation appears in a side panel. No new tabs, no rabbit holes.

Why we forget what we came for

Interruptions don’t erase intentions, they bury them. Working memory holds only a handful of items, and a new tab pushes the old one out.

Every attention-grabbing headline is another small exit, and each exit makes the way back a little harder to find.

    What it is, and what it isn’t.

    Intents is

    • Reactive, not intrusive
    • Context-first, not task-first
    • Minimal, opinionated and local-first
    • Built for short memory, attention volatility and cognitive overload

    Intents is not

    • A productivity tracker
    • A task manager
    • A site blocker
    • A search engine
    • A motivational tool

    It won’t tell you what to do. It helps you remember why you were doing something.

    Reactive, never demandingMinimal over feature-richTemporary over persistentCalm over cleverUser-initiated, always

    Nothing leaves your device.

    • No accounts
    • No analytics
    • No tracking
    • No telemetry
    • No remote code execution

    All data stays on your device. Optional external services, such as AI providers, are configured by you and operate under their own policies.

    The source is open, so all of this can be verified.
